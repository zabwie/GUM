To understand why a pendulum clock runs slow in a steadily accelerating train, we must look at how the pendulum perceives gravity and how its path changes when the train moves along a curve.

### 1. The Effective Gravity Direction

In a stationary frame, a pendulum hangs vertically under the influence of Earth’s gravity ($g$). However, inside an accelerating train, the pendulum is in a non-inertial reference frame. To an observer inside the train, it appears as if there is a fictitious force acting on the pendulum bob, opposite to the direction of the train’s acceleration.

Let’s define the vectors:
- $\vec{g}$: The real gravitational acceleration, pointing downward.
- $\vec{a}$: The acceleration of the train.
